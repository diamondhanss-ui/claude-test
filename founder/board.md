# Board summary: FareFinder (working name)

## 1. The vote
**2 PASS, 1 FUND IF.** Average score: **2.3 / 10** (Offers 2, Monopoly 2, Product 3).

The board does not fund the idea as pitched. A PASS is a result, not a failure: as described, there is no payer, no edge and no chosen data source.

## 2. Risks raised by more than one member (read these first)
1. **No edge beyond "cheapest" (all three).** A thin LLM wrapper is a convenience, and existing fare-search and alert tools may already do this. The Offers lens calls it a commodity, the Monopoly lens says it is easy to copy, and the Product lens says no single thing is done brilliantly.
2. **No flight-data provider chosen (all three).** Without one, nobody knows whether the tool can return real, current, bookable fares, what they cost per query, or what stops a rival with the same data.
3. **No revenue and no distribution (Offers and Monopoly).** A free tool pays for neither LLM and data costs nor acquiring the first 100 users.
4. **Trust in the answer (Offers and Product).** A confidently wrong or stale fare ends the product on first use.

## 3. Conditions (checklist for the rest of the pack)
- [ ] Name the one thing it does that a fare-search site cannot, in one sentence (Product, Monopoly).
- [ ] Launch on one chat platform, not three (Product).
- [ ] Choose a real data source with known access terms and per-query cost (all). -> /founder-cfo, /founder-competitors
- [ ] Show each reply carries the price and the date checked, and matches the seller site (Product).
- [ ] Define the path to a bookable link and what happens when a fare is stale (Product).
- [ ] Map what flexible travelers use today, what it costs, what they complain about, and the gap (all). -> /founder-competitors
- [ ] Measure who would switch and what would flip a no (Offers, Product). -> /founder-consumer
- [ ] Add a revenue model or a stated reason it stays free, covering cost per user (Offers, Monopoly). -> /founder-pricing, /founder-cfo
- [ ] Pick a narrow first market (one route, city or trip type) and a way to reach the first 100 users (Monopoly). -> /founder-marketing
- [ ] Offer something beyond price, and cost any guarantee (Offers). -> /founder-offer, /founder-cfo
- [ ] Prove the fares it finds are real and lower than existing tools (Offers, Product).

## 4. Where the members disagree
The Product lens gave FUND IF because the pitch is clear and chat is a low-friction way in. The Offers and Monopoly lenses passed because clarity does not make a business: nothing yet pays for it or protects it. The gap is about whether this is a product worth refining or a business worth funding.

## 5. The strongest version the board can see
A narrow, one-job tool for one kind of trip (for example, one origin and a handful of flexible-date routes), on one chat platform, where every answer shows the fare, the date it was checked and a link to book. Its edge would be a measurable result, such as fares verified lower than the common tools, rather than plain-language input. Revenue could come from alerts or a share of verified savings, once a real buyer test shows someone will pay. This differs from the pitch: it is narrower, it must prove its fares, and it is not simply free and general.
