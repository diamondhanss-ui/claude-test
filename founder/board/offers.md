# Offers lens

Score: 2/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- The pain is real and easy to state: flexible leisure travelers want the lowest fare and can move their dates, which is the group that benefits most from a date-flexible search.
- Effort asked of the buyer is low: describe criteria in plain language in a chat app they already use.
- Time to result could be fast, since a chat answer takes seconds.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No revenue and no one paying.** The brief says free tool, no revenue. This lens cannot score an offer nobody pays for: there is no price, no buyer with money at stake, and no way to test whether the market spends. A free tool is a hobby until someone pays.
2. **It is a commodity, and the only edge is "cheapest".** The brief names no difference beyond finding low fares, which is the one thing the lens says never to compete on. Nothing in the brief makes a buyer feel stupid saying no: no extra outcome, no guarantee, no bundle. Whether existing fare-search and price-alert products already do this is unknown (open question, see below).
3. **Likelihood is unproven and the data source is unchosen.** The buyer has no reason to believe a chat wrapper finds fares better than what they already use. No flight-data provider is chosen, so whether the tool can return real, bookable, current prices at all is unknown. A wrapper that invents or misquotes fares destroys trust on the first use.

Also weak: the market score. Pain is moderate (a saving, not a crisis), the buyers' willingness to pay is unmeasured, and how to reach them and whether the segment is growing are not stated.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who pays, and for what? What is the first thing a user would hand money over for? (/founder-consumer)
- What do flexible travelers use today, what do those products charge and what do their reviewers complain about? Where is the gap? (/founder-competitors)
- Which flight-data provider, and what does each query cost you? Does the cost per search exceed anything you could earn per user? (/founder-cfo)
- How big is the segment of flexible leisure travelers you can reach, and how many would switch from their current tool? (/founder-competitors for market size, /founder-consumer for switching)
- What is the promise beyond "cheapest"? What would you guarantee, and could you afford to honor it? (/founder-cfo for the cost of a guarantee)
- Would a buyer pay to have this done for them (alerts, booking, savings share), and what would that price be? (/founder-consumer)
- What proof can you show that the fares it finds are real and lower? (/founder-competitors for the benchmark against existing tools)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable. Vote is PASS. To move to FUND IF, the founder would need: a paid offer with a named price and a measured buy rate from a real buyer test; a stated reason it beats existing fare tools beyond price; and a data source with known per-query cost showing positive margin. None of these exist in the brief.
