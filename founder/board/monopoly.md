# Monopoly lens

Score: 2/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- Clear, narrow job: find the cheapest fare for a flexible leisure traveler. That is a real, common pain, and the target user is specific.
- Chat delivery (Slack/Telegram/WhatsApp) avoids app-store friction and is a plausible low-cost way to reach people where they already are.
- Timing has some merit: LLMs now make plain-language, flexible-date search easy to build. The same fact also makes it easy to copy (see below).

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No durable edge and a crowded field.** The brief describes a thin wrapper around an LLM. The Monopoly lens looks for proprietary technology roughly ten times better than the next option, network effects, scale economies or a brand, and the brief names none. Flexible-date, cheapest-fare search already exists in established flight search and fare-alert products, and a copycat could ship this in a month. Who sells this to the same buyer, and how they compare, is unknown (open question, answered by /founder-competitors).
2. **No source of cheap flight data, and so no moat.** No data provider is chosen. Whoever supplies the fares can sell the same answer to anyone, and the wrapper adds nothing they cannot add. Access terms, rate limits and cost per query are unknown (open question, answered by /founder-competitors for what rivals use and /founder-cfo for per-query cost).
3. **No distribution and no business model.** The brief gives no way to reach the first 100 users and no cost for each. A free tool with no revenue leaves nothing to pay for acquisition or for LLM and data costs, and no small market it can own first. Whether anyone would switch from what they use now is untested (open question, answered by /founder-consumer). Cost to serve one user and the funding needed are unknown (open question, answered by /founder-cfo).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Engineering: what does this do ten times better than a search engine's flexible-date view or a fare alert? If the answer is "plain-language input", that is a convenience, not a breakthrough.
- Secret: what do you know about finding cheap fares that others do not?
- Monopoly: which small group of travelers could you own first (one route, one city, one trip type) and how many are they?
- Distribution: how do the first 100 users arrive, and what does each cost?
- Durability: what still holds in ten years once every travel site has its own LLM search?
- People: why is this the right team for flight data and travel search?
- Who pays, and for what, given the tool is free?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable, the vote is PASS. The idea would be worth re-presenting only with: a chosen data source with known cost and access terms, a proprietary edge (for example, data or a method rivals lack) shown to beat the next option by a wide margin on a measurable result, a narrow first market with a named way to reach it, and a revenue model that covers the cost per user.
